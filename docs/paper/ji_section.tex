\subsection{Study JI: Do More Views Beat a Second Judge on Fresh Worlds? Study JC: A Fast Judge That Decides Where to Look}\label{sec:moved-ji}
The factor analysis of study JH (post hoc, one image set) found that more look-around views remove more of the gate's misses than a second judge does. Study JI (registered and stamped before any image was rendered; thresholds frozen from XD, nothing fitted) replays it on \JIFire{} fresh small, partly hidden fires and \JIClean{} fire-free twins, six views each (F, L, R, L2, R2, N), at the image level.
\emph{Claims.} JI1: the gate on six views misses at most 8 of the fires: \JISix{} (\JISixRate, upper \JISixUpper): \JIOneHolds. JI2: six views miss fewer fires than the standard three (\JIThree{}; \JIOnlyThree{} fires missed only with three views, \JIOnlySix{} only with six, one-sided exact McNemar $p=\JIP$): \JITwoHolds. JI3: at most 5 false stops with six views, \JISixFS{} (three views: \JIThreeFS): \JIThreeHolds. JI4: gate plus the InternVL3 veto on six views misses at most 3 with at most 6 false stops, \JIVetoMiss{} and \JIVetoFS: \JIFourHolds. With all four judges as vetoes: \JIAllMiss{} missed, \JIAllFS{} false stops (descriptive).
 \emph{Diversity of the misses (post hoc, \texttt{scripts/diversity\_measures.py}).} The classical oracle-based diversity measures make ``a second judge adds almost nothing'' quantitative. With the standard three views the gate and InternVL3 both miss \DivJIThreeDF{} of the \DivJIThreeClusters{} fire worlds, three times the \DivJIThreeInd{} expected if their misses were independent (Q statistic \DivJIThreeQ; all four judges: double-fault rates \DivJIThreeAll); with six views \DivJISixDF{} against \DivJISixInd{} (Q \DivJISixQ), and on XD counted by base world \DivXDThreeDF{} against \DivXDThreeInd{}. The misses are shared, which is what a model-family-correlated pool predicts~\cite{bugaud2026hiddenclones,kohli2026ninejudges}.
\emph{Reading.} The view count replicates, and it is the registered form of the post hoc finding of study JH: on fresh worlds the gate misses \JIThree{} fires with the three standard views and \JISix{} with six, never the other way round (\JIOnlyThree{} against \JIOnlySix{}, $p=\JIP$), at no false stop in either case. The veto of a second judge from another family moves the three-view count by one fire and the six-view count by one (\JIVetoMiss{} with the selected judge); only the union of all four judges reaches \JIAllMiss{} and pays \JIAllFS{} false stops, i.e. its price is availability. The extra poses are what catches the fires: the 0.8\,m side views carry most of them. The paper's reading therefore stays: a second opinion on an image adds little to a better observation of the scene. Limits: image level, one simulator, one fire geometry, thresholds frozen from XD, and the extra views cost driving time (study JK measures that in the closed loop).

\textbf{Study JC (the judge's job is speed).} Study JH used the judge as a redundant veto. JC gives it a role the gate does not have: a sentinel on the three standard views that decides whether the three extra views are worth driving; it can neither approve nor refuse, so a sentinel false alarm costs time, not a delivery (Proposition~2 and the gate's false-stop budget are unchanged). The sentinel is the judge with the lowest median scoring latency in JI (\JCPrimary{}, \JCLatSent\,s per image; InternVL3: \JCLatIntern\,s). On the same worlds, the gate misses \JCThreeMiss{} with three views and \JCSixMiss{} with six; with the sentinel as trigger it misses \JCSentMiss{} at a mean of \JCSentViews{} views per clean mission (three views: 3, six: 6; extra views triggered on \JCTrigFire{} of the fires and \JCTrigClean{} of the clean worlds), \JCSentFS{} false stops; the gate's own low-score trigger gives \JCOwnMiss{} at \JCOwnViews{} views. Claims: JC1 (misses within 3 of always-six) \JCOneHolds; JC2 (at most 4.0 views on clean worlds) \JCTwoHolds; JC3 (at most 5 false stops) \JCThreeHolds. Predictions: fewer misses than always three \JCPredOneHolds; faster than always six in the estimated extra time (\JCSentSecs\,s against \JCSixSecs\,s per clean mission, drive time borrowed from GL, not measured) \JCPredTwoHolds.
Cascades that call an expensive model only when a cheap one is unsure~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt}, with early abstention by the small members~\cite{zellinger2025abstention}, and minority-veto ensembles of judges that otherwise rarely flag errors~\cite{jain2025agreeableness} are the designs JC borrows; averaging over augmented views can flip correct predictions~\cite{shanmugam2020tta}, one reason we fuse views by a maximum.
\emph{Reading.} JC1 fails, and the failure is informative. The sentinel (\JCPrimary{}) fires on most of the fires and spares most of the clean worlds, yet the gate then misses \JCSentMiss{} fires against \JCSixMiss{} with all six views: a fire that needs the extra views is, by construction, one that the standard views show poorly, and a different model family does not see it either. The gate's own score is a better trigger and is free: at a score of 0.01 on the standard views the gate misses \JCOwnMiss{} at \JCOwnViews{} mean views (estimated \JCOwnSecs\,s of extra time per clean mission, against \JCSentSecs\,s for the sentinel and \JCSixSecs\,s for always six). Speed did not decide it either: the fastest judge needs \JCLatSent\,s per image (four passes) against \JCLatIntern\,s for the selected InternVL3, close to the gate's own 2\,s per view. A caution from outside points the same way: detector confidence has been found to signal that a category is present rather than that the target is visible~\cite{he2026detector}, so a score-based trigger needs the check we made (JI's misses with and without the trigger). Escalating when models disagree has the same weakness: correlated failures leave no disagreement to act on~\cite{mcdonnell2026agreement}. We therefore do not claim a role for the second judge in choosing views; the remaining role in which speed matters, watching the route while the robot drives, is not tested. Limits: image level, drive time borrowed from GL (not measured), thresholds frozen from XD.
