\textbf{Machine-checked monotonicity (I-23).} We transcribed the code behind Proposition~2 into z3 (\texttt{scripts/verify\_advice\_smt.py}): the plan fold \texttt{advice.plan} with \texttt{assert\_monotone}; the approval predicate as the mission node composes it (\texttt{apply\_memory\_caution}, \texttt{peek\_hazard}, \texttt{combine\_with\_look\_around}, adaptive and scrutiny views; scores are reals plus an \emph{invalid} flag, labels and the suspicion trigger are free); and the loop over \texttt{stations.next\_candidate}. Advice is unconstrained: any list of any length (an inductive invariant of the fold), malformed items included. All 134 queries are \texttt{unsat} (32\,s on one core; $k\le8$ plan, $k\le6$ loop; the five contrast queries of the continue-after-hazard loop are \texttt{sat} by design):
(P2a)~approval with advice $\Rightarrow$ approval under the base plan $\Rightarrow$ approval under the v3.6 rule, on arbitrary frames; (P2b)~every delivery follows an approving check at that station, the order stays a permutation of all $k$ stations and the visits are a prefix of it, so an unsafe delivery implies $E_w$; (C)~at most $k$ visits, one extra visit per reorder (attained), at most six views per station (advice adds only the three scrutiny views), and an approval is lost to advice only through $p_h\ge$ the caution threshold or a flagged scrutiny view. Premises are shown satisfiable and the bounds tight.
\emph{Continue after a hazard (I-42, \texttt{--hazard-continue}, default off).} v3.6 stops at the first real hazard. The flag makes a real hazard (the standard rule on valid scores, front or side view) a refusal \emph{of that bay only}: no delivery from it, and the search goes on with the next candidate, which gets its own full check. We added a fourth outcome class (real hazard) to the loop model: a stop in v3.6, a move to the next candidate with the flag. For $k\le6$ the 35 further queries are \texttt{unsat}: every delivery follows an approving check at that bay; the visits are at most $k$, each bay once, a prefix of the order; a hazard-refused bay is never the delivery bay; an unsafe delivery still implies $E_w$; and if some bay approves and nothing else stops the search (low confidence, a broken view), the mission delivers, so a hazard cannot cost a delivery that another bay makes. Advice changes a bay's outcome only from approval to hazard refusal (\texttt{apply\_memory\_caution} and \texttt{combine\_with\_look\_around} are the identity on a refusal: a differential test on the real functions). Under that lemma advice of any order and any tightened set loses a delivery only if \emph{every} bay that approves without advice was tightened; with one tightened bay (a location item plus scrutiny) a second approving bay always delivers. The same loop without the flag gives the counterexample (5 expected \texttt{sat}), and three mutants (a hazard-refused bay read as approved, the next candidate skipped, the flag read but a hazard still stops) each have a z3 counterexample. The model agrees with the real node on 60 random outcome sequences with the flag on and off. \emph{Not covered:} exposure, since every further visit is another chance of a missed fire (also for a robot without advice that carries the flag); and the visit-order independence (A1), which the depot breaks (the arrival pose depends on the previous bay), so the control's approval of the clean bay does not transfer by the proof and study Y tests it.
\emph{Link to the code.} 9\,000 hypothesis inputs (boundary values, NaN, inf, bool, out-of-range scores, malformed and adversarial advice, naive base plans) are evaluated by the real functions and by the model and agree exactly (\texttt{tests/test\_i23\_smt\_refinement.py}); Proposition~2 also holds on the real code with the plan assertion off. Eleven injected bugs (advice loosens $\tau_h$, skips a check, marks a station verified, refuses unseen, drops a candidate; caution boundary or side views ignored; an invalid score read as clear) each give a z3 counterexample that makes the mutated code misbehave when replayed, and each is killed by the differential test; for relaxing interfaces z3 returns the two-world law and clairvoyant advice of the \emph{Necessity} argument.
\emph{Trusted, and not covered.} Trusted: z3, CPython and the transcription. The ROS node is not executed: its loop is a model read off the source (guarded by a tripwire test) and the decision functions are composed as in the node. Not covered: $\varepsilon$, scene text, the camera and weights, frame independence (A1), the Jev-role model, the destination parse, time beyond the counts above. Three findings lie outside the proposition (advice cannot cause them): the audit raised on a run refused at an early extra view, and NaN or boolean supply confidences were accepted; all three are fixed, fail closed. \emph{Reproduction.} \texttt{make verify} (host Python with \texttt{requirements-verify.txt}; no ROS, GPU or model weights) reruns every query, the differential and the mutants, and requires the same verdicts as the committed record; a fresh run on the authors' host took 4 minutes.
