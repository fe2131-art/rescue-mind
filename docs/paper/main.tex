\section{Introduction}
Relief logistics is a last-mile problem: goods move from local depots to the people who need them~\cite{balcik2008lastmile}, under
conditions in which records are stale and sites change by the hour~\cite{murphy2014disaster}. A robot that delivers whatever its
inventory says is at a station may hand a shelter a first-aid kit when the shelter asked for water, report a delivery from an empty shelf,
or, because an inventory says nothing about hazards, drive into a fire. A vision-language model (VLM) can often describe such situations without task-specific
training~\cite{liu2023reflect,duan2025aha,agia2025sentinel}. However, its outputs depend on option order and answer letters~\cite{zheng2024mcselectors,pezeshkpour2024order},
it hallucinates objects~\cite{li2023pope}, and its free text can be unparseable (we observed all three; Supp.~Sec.~S8).

A \emph{gate} (the pre-action decision, made by one \emph{check} per station) built on such a model has an \emph{error budget} (the chance that it approves a station it should refuse), and everything that makes a robot faster threatens it: memory of where
the water was (stale memory can be worse than none~\cite{sun2026memorylies}), a peer's report, the inventory, the operator's wording.
The question is therefore: \emph{how can a robot profit from inputs it did not perceive itself without letting them weaken its checks?}
Our answer is one chain, each link carried by one study:
(1)~the gate fails closed and is audited in closed loop (Sec.~\ref{sec:system}; E, T1);
(2)~the check's residual error $\varepsilon$ comes mostly from small, partly hidden fires that the front view often misses (T1, W);
(3)~a \emph{look-around} (two extra side views after a front approval) reduces $\varepsilon$ (G, and W at image level; adaptive views: W, I-20);
(4)~\emph{advice may only tighten}: anything not perceived at the station may reorder or tighten checks but never exceed the check's own error budget $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ (Prop.~2; memory: H and V; peers, operator text and inventory: A);
(5)~scene text (S) and realism (depot, X) lie outside the guarantee; \IfPendS{we do not claim them, and the study that would measure scene text (S) is registered and was not run at submission}{we measure them and do not claim them} (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-out}).
Our contribution is one sentence: \emph{\contribsent}
The guarantee is relative. We prove it and machine-check it with z3 against a transcription of the implementation, which differential testing links to the code (Prop.~2, Supp.~Sec.~S10), and we measure its price in time and refusals against naive and filter baselines in ground-truth-audited closed loop (study A\IfPendA{, registered and not yet run at submission}{}).
Two supporting results are not part of that claim:
\textbf{(a)}~the look-around lowers $\varepsilon$ (the bound holds for any $\varepsilon$; the look-around makes it smaller);
\textbf{(b)}~an auditable, pre-registered evaluation that reports its failed claims.
The principle itself is old (Sec.~\ref{sec:related}); we do not claim it as new.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Verification and VLM gates.}
REFLECT~\cite{liu2023reflect}, AHA~\cite{duan2025aha} and success detectors~\cite{du2023successdetectors} explain failures after the fact; Sentinel~\cite{agia2025sentinel}, FAIL-Detect~\cite{xu2025faildetect},
Code-as-Monitor~\cite{zhou2025codeasmonitor} and an LLM anomaly monitor~\cite{sinha2024realtime} watch policies at run time.
The closest work we know is VAP-TAMP~\cite{oloo2026vaptamp}: a VLM answers paraphrased yes/no questions before and after each action, a vote updates the planner's state, and the camera moves if the view is insufficient.
To our knowledge, ours differs in its closed-set next-token scores, a fail-closed conjunction (a failed check or an extra view can only refuse or revoke), an independent velocity guard, advice limited to reordering and tightening, and pre-registered closed-loop audits against ground truth.
KnowNo~\cite{ren2023knowno} and SayCan~\cite{ichter2023saycan} also score multiple-choice answers; we refuse or search under uncertainty and add permuted-option agreement~\cite{zheng2024mcselectors,pezeshkpour2024order,robinson2023mcqa}.
Simplex~\cite{seto1998simplex,sha2001simplicity}, shields~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding} and control barrier functions~\cite{ames2019cbf} keep a simple verified layer between a complex decision maker and the actuators; unlike VLA models~\cite{zitkovich2023rt2,kim2025openvla}, our VLM never commands motion.

\textbf{Untrusted inputs to embodied agents.}
Jailbreaks steer LLM-controlled robots through the instruction (RoboPAIR~\cite{robey2025jailbreaking}, BadRobot~\cite{zhang2025badrobot}); printed text in the camera view hijacks VLM-controlled robots~\cite{burbano2026chai,samarakoon2026paper}; poisoned memory steers LLM agents~\cite{chen2024agentpoison}. Robot defenses mostly filter or certify the plan (RoboGuard~\cite{ravichandran2026guardrails}).
Structurally, our rule is a one-way relaxation of integrity non-interference~\cite{biba1977integrity,denning1976lattice} as enforced for software agents by CaMeL and Fides~\cite{debenedetti2025camel,costa2025fides}, and it reads as an algorithm with predictions: advice may lower cost but never the worst-case guarantee~\cite{lykouris2021competitive,dinitz2021faster}. Unlike strict non-interference, correct advice still saves time; unlike filters, the guarantee does not depend on recognizing advice as malicious.
Concurrent proofs of non-escalation and attenuation-only authority~\cite{chepuri2026authguardr,trantruong2026delegateguard,kravchenko2026appa,akewar2026safecommit} have no stochastic perception gate (Supp.~Sec.~S3). What is new, to our knowledge as of October 2026, is the combination of three elements: a probabilistic perception gate on a simulated mobile robot, a bound relative to the check's own error with a measured availability cost, and a ground-truth-audited comparison with naive and filter baselines\IfPendA{ (registered, study A)}{}.

\textbf{Memory and active perception.}
Semantic memory~\cite{anwar2025remembr,wang2025karma} and scene graphs~\cite{hughes2022hydra,gu2024conceptgraphs} \emph{answer} or \emph{plan}~\cite{rana2023sayplan}; GLOVE~\cite{yin2026glove} and VLM-DEWM~\cite{tang2026vlmdewm} check memory against fresh observations; object search with belief priors~\cite{aydemir2013active,wandzel2019mos,zeng2020slim,zhou2023esc} and semi-static maps~\cite{mason2012objectworld,rosen2016lifelong} trust detections and do not bound the risk of stale memory at a pre-action gate. We use memory to decide \emph{where and how carefully to verify}.
Moving the sensor to resolve ambiguity is classic~\cite{bajcsy1988active,bajcsy2018revisiting}, and agents explore until a VLM is confident~\cite{ren2024exploreuntil}; our look-around is deliberately simple: two fixed side views used only to \emph{revoke} an approval.
We register studies in advance~\cite{nosek2018preregistration} and report Wilson intervals~\cite{wilson1927probable} and exact McNemar tests~\cite{mcnemar1947note}.

\section{A Fail-Closed Gate and Its Error Budget}\label{sec:system}
\textbf{Platform and mission.} A differential-drive robot with a 2D lidar and a mast camera (640$\times$480) runs in Gazebo Harmonic with ROS~2 Jazzy~\cite{macenski2022ros2,koenig2004gazebo} (Fig.~\ref{fig:system}). The arena has two supply stations (water, medicine) and a shelter; the depot of Sec.~\ref{sec:res-out} has four stations.
The VLM, Qwen3.5-9B~\cite{qwen2026qwen35} in FP16 on one Tesla V100, was chosen on development data (Supp.~Sec.~S2); no cloud API is used. A request (Japanese or English) is parsed into a supply and a destination by two closed-set VLM questions and an explicit rule; an unclear request is refused before the robot moves. The robot drives to a station, aims, waits until it is stationary and captures fresh frames.

\subsection{Fail-Closed Check}\label{sec:checkpoint}
For an image $x$ and requested supply $s^\ast$, the VLM is asked two differently worded questions about the platform with permuted options ($q_1$: A water / B first-aid kit / C nothing or other; $q_2$: A neither / B first-aid kit / C water) and, once per image, the hazard question $q_h$: ``Do you see any fire, flames, or smoke? Yes/No''. We take the softmax over the log-sum-exp of the next-token logits of each answer symbol's tokenizer variants, giving $p_k(s)$ and $p_h$; these are scores to be thresholded, not calibrated probabilities. The robot approves iff
\begin{equation}
\bigwedge_{k=1,2}\Big[\arg\max_s p_k(s)=s^\ast \;\wedge\; p_k(s^\ast)\ge\taus\Big]\;\wedge\;p_h<\tauh,
\label{eq:rule}\end{equation}
with $\taus=0.5$ and $\tauh=0.05$, fixed on development data. The hazard wording names small, partly hidden fire and smoke, and the question is also asked on three enlarged crops ($p_h$ is the maximum; this configuration is ``v3.6''; the candidates are in Supp.~Sec.~S12).
The asymmetry is deliberate: a missed fire can hurt people, whereas a false alarm only stops the robot. Every failure refuses: inference errors, non-finite logits, unknown labels, mismatching image hashes and a model not on the allow-list all end in a refusal, which is followed by a 2.5\,s zero-velocity hold that the audit checks.
After a wrong or missing item the robot checks the other station once with the same rule; after a hazard it never searches but stops and reports.
In the spirit of Simplex, the mission node only \emph{requests} velocities, and an independent guard is the sole publisher of wheel commands. The guard publishes zero unless camera, lidar and odometry are fresh and the front sector is clear. Algorithm~1 (Supp.~Sec.~S12) lists one mission.

\subsection{Where the Error Lives, and the Look-Around}\label{sec:fix}
\textbf{The error budget.} A station is \emph{must-refuse} if it holds the wrong or no supply, or a \emph{perceivable} fire (a rendered fire-only mask has at least 614 pixels in at least one of three views: front, left, right). Let $\varepsilon_s$ be the probability, over the world distribution, that station $s$ is must-refuse \emph{and} the check approves it; frames at a station do not depend on the visiting order in a static world, so $\varepsilon_s$ does not either. Held-out images (T1, 600) show where $\varepsilon$ comes from. The supply check made \TSupplyFA{} false approvals on \TSupplyN{} must-refuse images without fire (\TSupplyFACI). All registered false approvals came from the \TFireCorrectN{} images with the correct supply and a visible fire (\TFireCorrectFA{} approved under the registered rule), in which the fire was partly hidden behind the supply. The registered claim of fewer than 2\% false approvals was therefore \emph{not} supported. Better wording and crops, chosen on development data, did not fix this in held-out or closed-loop confirmation (T2, F: Supp.~Sec.~S12): a hidden fire carries no pixels to reason about.

\textbf{Look-around.} After the front check approves, the robot performs a \emph{look-around}: it drives to two poses 0.4\,m to the left and right of the checkpoint, aims at the station center, waits, captures fresh frames and asks the hazard question. Any side view that shows a hazard turns the approval into a hazard refusal, without a search (Fig.~\ref{fig:lookaround}). With side views $V=\{L,R\}$, $A = A_{\mathrm{front}} \wedge \bigwedge_{v\in V} [\,p_{h}^{(v)} < \tauh\,]$, and the audit recomputes every side decision. Mask renders show that the same fires cover 4\,600--22\,000 pixels from the side (Fig.~\ref{fig:firesize}).
Campaign G (pre-registered, paired, new worlds): in the \GPairs{} worlds whose fire was perceivable, the front-only gate delivered despite the fire in \GWrongB{} of them and the gate with look-around in \GWrongC{} (exact McNemar $p=\GMcNemarP$; \GDiscB{} discordant worlds favored look-around, \GDiscC{} the other arm), with \GFalseStopB{} and \GFalseStopC{} false hazard refusals in fire-free runs, at a median cost of \GLookCost\,s.
\textbf{Plug-in budget.} With look-around, $\hat\varepsilon=\GEpsHat$ for this hard class (Wilson upper bound \GEpsUpper), so $k\hat\varepsilon=\GKEpsHat$ for $k=2$ (upper bound \GKEpsUpper); the budget is small, not zero, which is why the guarantee below is relative; W (below) tightens it to \WMissLARate{} (upper \WMissLAUpper) on 150 new worlds. \textbf{Closed loop (GR, GC, GD).} On \GRWorlds{} fresh worlds the look-around delivered into a fire in \GRVThreeSix{} (upper \GRVThreeSixUpper; front-only \GRFront, $p=\GRMcNemar$). A threshold of $0.01$ from the site's largest fire-free score gave \GCCal{} against \GCVThreeSix{} on \GCWorlds{} new worlds (\GCFalseStops{} false stops; paired $p=\GCMcNemar$), and the same recipe at the depot ($0.028$) gave \DepCalCal{} against \DepCalVThreeSix{}: it holds the false-stop rate, not the miss rate, since hard fires score inside the clean-world tail; widening the views to 0.8\,m raised that tail past the default (study GV stopped at $\tau=\WideStopTau$, Supp.~Sec.~S12-M). Pooled, v3.6 delivers at \DepCalPool{} (upper \DepCalPoolUpper). A conformal bound caps false stops at $1/(m+1)=1.3\%$ per mission (Supp.~Sec.~S12).
\textbf{Image-level replication (W).} Study W (registered; frozen v3.6, nothing tuned) replays the check on \WNPerc{} new small, partly hidden fires, each rendered from the front and both side poses (every one shows at least 614 fire pixels from some view), and on 75 fire-free twins. Front-only approved \WMissFront{} of them (\WMissFrontRate; \WMissFrontSide{} of the fires that only a side view shows); look-around approved \WMissLA{} (\WMissLARate, Wilson \WMissLACI; worlds \WMissedWorlds{}, all side-only fires). The registered bound (upper bound $\le15\%$) is therefore \WClaimEps{}, against an upper bound of \GEpsUpper{} in G, at \WExtraStop{} extra false refusals on the twins (\WTwinCA{} correct approvals; cost claim \WClaimCost{}). The rate is a mixture: look-around missed \WMissLAFront{} fires that the front view shows and \textbf{\WMissLASide{} side-only fires (\WMissLASideRate, Wilson \WMissLASideCI)}, the class that a hidden fire belongs to. \WMissFrontVis{} of the front-only misses were fires the front view does show: small and partly hidden, not invisible. Scope: every fire stands straight behind the requested supply (0.55--0.75\,m back, lateral $\pm0.15$\,m), so perceivability from some view is a property of the generator (\WNPerc{} of \WNPerc{}); lateral, low and occluded fires are not sampled, and a fire that no view shows is excluded by the label (\WNotVisible, upper \WNotVisibleUpper), not estimated. The order-independent $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ from these worlds is \WEpsAnyTwo{} (\WEpsAnyTwoCI) for $k=2$ and \WEpsAnyFour{} (\WEpsAnyFourCI) for $k=4$ with look-around (front-only \WEpsAnyTwoFront{}, \WEpsAnyFourFront{}), from independent random worlds rather than $k$ stations of one site; the upper bound at $k=4$, \WEpsAnyFourUpper, lies above the 15\% line registered for one station. The closest prior mechanisms did not separate from ours at the same full-image input (\WNImages{} test images, \WNDeny{} must-refuse; ours-full approved \WFAOursFull{}). A five-paraphrase vote~\cite{oloo2026vaptamp} approved \WFAVote{} (Holm $p=\WVoteHolm$); a conformal set~\cite{ren2023knowno} at $\alpha=0.10$ gave the same decisions as ours-full, and at $\alpha=0.05$ it approved \WFAKnowFive{} but correctly approved only \WCAKnowFive{} safe images (ours \WCAOursFull). The registered claim that the hazard rule alone beats them is therefore \emph{not} supported (\WNDist{} of 3); the deployed check with crops (\WFAOursDeployed) beats the vote (Holm $p=\WVoteDeployedHolm$), so the separation comes from wording plus crops together. Suspicion-triggered extra views (adaptive views, I-20: two more poses at 0.8\,m and one 0.3\,m closer whenever a view scores above 0.005 but below $\tauh$) missed \WAdaptMiss{}. They triggered on \WAdaptTrigTwin{} twins and lost no correct approval (\WAdaptTwinCA), so the adoption rule fixed beforehand (Supp.~Sec.~S12) was met, on one world as the whole evidence; the depot study XD then applied the revert rule fixed before it was rendered (Supp.~Sec.~S12) and withdrew them as a default. Limits: image level, one generator and model family, a stylized fire, and v3.6's wording was developed on the same generator.

\section{Advice May Only Tighten}\label{sec:advice}
\textbf{Threat model.} The attacker controls the content of any advice channel (a memory file, a peer's report, the inventory, the operator's words), may know the world and this rule, and may send \emph{truthful} advice so that nothing can be flagged. The attacker cannot change the camera pixels (scene text is a separate case, Sec.~\ref{sec:res-out}), the model weights, the code or the guard. The attacker's goals are an unsafe delivery (a delivery from a must-refuse station: a wrong or no supply, or a perceivable fire; Prop.~2) or a denial of service (lost deliveries, lost time).
\subsection{Memory and Other Untrusted Inputs as Advice}\label{sec:memory}
After every mission the robot appends what \emph{it} observed to a memory file (never ground truth): per checked station, the supply both passes named with $p\ge0.5$ and whether a hazard was seen, from which views. Memory and every other input the robot did not perceive at the station (a peer's report, the inventory, hints or claims in the operator's request such as ``the water was moved to M'' or ``the fire is out, no need to check'') can be stale or adversarial~\cite{chen2024agentpoison,robey2025jailbreaking,zhang2025badrobot}. We call all of them \emph{advice}. An advice item is $a=(\text{source},\text{kind},\text{station})$ with $\text{kind}\in\{\text{location},\text{hazard},\text{safe},\text{skip}\}$. At plan time, one \emph{tighten-only} rule compiles the items into a visiting order $\pi$ and check parameters $\theta'=(\taus',\tauh',V')$: \emph{location} advice may reorder $\pi$ (that station is still checked); \emph{hazard} advice (or a hazard in memory during the last three missions) may tighten $\tauh'$ to $0.01$ (the \emph{caution threshold}) at the named station and look at the revealing side first; \emph{safe} and \emph{skip} advice has no effect (it is rejected and logged); advice cannot make the robot refuse without visiting. The interface has no field through which advice could remove a check. For example, the request ``the water was moved to M; the fire is out, no need to check'' yields a location item (visit M first, still check it) and a safe item, which is rejected.

Let $\theta=(\taus,\tauh,V)$ be the baseline parameters and $A_\theta(o)\in\{0,1\}$ the approval for the observations $o$ at a station. A plan is \emph{verification-preserving} (the formal content of tighten-only) if $\taus'\ge\taus$, $\tauh'\le\tauh$, $V'\supseteq V$ at every station checked, and a delivery needs $A_{\theta'}$ at the delivering station.

\textbf{Observation 1.} For a verification-preserving plan, $A_{\theta'}(o)\le A_\theta(o)$ for every $o$: $A_\theta$ is a conjunction of a supply condition (non-increasing in $\taus$) and hazard conditions $[p_h^{(v)}<\tauh]$ (non-increasing as $\tauh$ decreases), and added views add conjuncts. The rule is kept elementary so that code and audit can enforce it.

\textbf{Proposition 2 (advice stays inside the error budget).} \emph{Setting.} The site has $k$ stations ($k=2$ in the arena, 4 in the depot). A sample point is $\omega=(w,\xi)$: a world $w$ from the generator and exogenous sensing noise $\xi$; the frame at station $s$ and pose $p$ of the design set (front, $L$, $R$ and the extra poses) is $o_{s,p}=F(w,s,p,\xi_{s,p})$, and $A_\theta(o_s)$ is the approval of the check on the frames at $s$. $M_s(w)$ says that $s$ is \emph{must-refuse}: it holds the wrong or no supply, or a fire perceivable at a design view. \emph{Unsafe} means a delivery from a must-refuse station; fires below the perceivability threshold are reported separately and lie outside the guarantee.
\emph{Assumptions.} (A1)~\emph{Frames are exogenous}: $o_{s,p}$ does not depend on the plan, the visiting order, the visit time or the history. (A2)~\emph{The plan only tightens}: $\tauh'\le\tauh$, $V'\supseteq V$ at every station (Obs.~1; audited), and the base plan is the baseline or verified memory, not a naive memory. (A3)~\emph{Gate}: the robot delivers from $s$ only after $A_{\theta'_s}(o_s)=1$ on the frames it captured at $s$ (the mission node; the delivery interlock, if on). (A4)~Advice does not influence $(w,\xi)$: the adversary may be any function of $\omega$ (clairvoyant) and adaptive across missions, but cannot change $w$, $\xi$, the model, the code or the guard.
\emph{Claim.} Let $E=\bigcup_{s\le k}\{M_s(w)\wedge A_\theta(o_s)=1\}$, $\varepsilon_s=\Pr[M_s\wedge A_\theta(o_s)=1]$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}=\Pr[E]$. For every mission and every advice map,
\[\Pr[\text{unsafe}]\;\le\;\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}\;\le\;\min\Bigl(1,\sum_{s=1}^{k}\varepsilon_s\Bigr),\qquad\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}\ge\max_s\varepsilon_s,\]
and over $m$ missions $\Pr[\exists\,\text{unsafe}]\le\min(1,m\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}})$ (equal to $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ if the frames repeat). $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ depends on the site and the check but not on the visiting order or the advice. The bound is attained by a clairvoyant attacker who may name any non-default station first; a duplicate supply is needed only for the control to have risk~0, so that advice \emph{raises} the risk. Image-level estimates of $\varepsilon_s$ hold at the design poses. Without the tightening condition (A2) the bound fails: for an interface that can make the gate approve a frame that the check alone refuses, the unsafe rate is at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$, with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rel}}$ the mass of must-refuse stations whose frames the advice can relax. Tighten-only is necessary under two premises: some relaxed frame can be produced at a must-refuse station (no sound certificate of safety exists) and an approval leads to a delivery; then a world law and a clairvoyant advisor push the unsafe rate above $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ (Supp.~Sec.~S10). A trusted certificate would void the first premise. The naive and filtered baselines relax every refused frame they skip or fail to filter, so they have no such bound. The proposition is machine-checked against a transcription of the implementation, not on the code itself: z3 proves the implication and that no delivery occurs without a check at that station, for advice lists of any length and arbitrary frames; the transcription is linked to the real functions by differential testing on 9\,000 adversarial inputs, and all 11 injected relaxing bugs are caught (Supp.~Sec.~S10). The model does not execute the ROS node.
\emph{Sketch} (full proof: Supp.~Sec.~S10). Fix $\omega$ and an advice set. If the robot delivers from a must-refuse $s$, then $A_{\theta'_s}(o_s)=1$ (A3); the supply conjunct is unchanged, $V\subseteq V'$ and $p_h<\tauh'\Rightarrow p_h<\tauh$ (A2), so $A_\theta(o_s)=1$ on the same frames (A1) and $\omega\in E$. The inclusion holds for every $\omega$ whatever the advice depends on (A4); Boole's inequality gives the sum.
\emph{Without A1} (depot: arrival pose and smoke phase depend on the history $h$) replace $A_\theta(o_s)$ in $E$ by $\exists h\in H_s:A_\theta(o^h_s)$ over the reachable histories, so $\varepsilon$ becomes a supremum. An average over histories, such as the stationary value we measure, estimates only a lower bound of that supremum and is therefore not a bound.

\textbf{Relative, not absolute.} The guarantee caps what advice can add to the check's own error budget (Sec.~\ref{sec:fix}); it does not bound that error itself, and it does not say that the risk with advice equals the risk of the memory-free robot, whose own order (the inventory) is advice too. Advice that changes \emph{which} station is checked changes \emph{exposure}: an attacker can steer the robot first to the station with the largest $\varepsilon_s$ (truthful location advice cannot be filtered), which $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}$ accounts for. The robot may also be driven toward a station that is really on fire; the guarantee concerns delivery, not travel. We report exposure rather than treating it as noise (Sec.~\ref{sec:res-a}).

\textbf{Price.} The bounds are per mission, against the robot without advice and with the same inventory order. The plan applies at most one location item, so wrong location advice adds at most one extra station check ($\le W$; Supp.~Table~S5), plus the scrutiny time $S$ if scrutiny is on (paid even when the advice is right). If the advised station really burns, the hazard refusal is a lost delivery, not a delay. A fire-free station under the caution threshold is falsely refused with probability at most $n_s\bar\delta$ ($n_s=3$ views, or 6 under scrutiny; $\bar\delta=\KCautFAUp$ is the Wilson upper bound of the per-view false-alarm rate, study K): \KCautThreeBoole{} and \KCautSixBoole{} (Boole). A soft refusal costs one more visit and saves the delivery only if another station holds the supply (the depot). False advice costs a bounded loss; truthful advice that points at a burning station can lose the whole mission (A-steer), because v3.6 ends the search at a refused hazard (a design choice, not a consequence of Prop.~2). The opt-in flag \texttt{--hazard-continue} refuses only that station and checks the next (proved; study Y, \YN{} runs: \YSLostScr{} deliveries lost with scrutiny, but \YFLostScr{} of \YFPairs{} on fresh worlds (YF4 failed); tighten-only still delivered into the XC05 fire in \YMonoMiss{} of 3, a perception miss; the price is exposure: \YEUnsafeHc{} unsafe deliveries when a hidden second fire waits at the next station, bound \YEUnsafeHcUpper, YE; Supp.~Sec.~S12). Two optional hardenings stay tighten-only: \emph{soft refusal} continues the search after a caution-only refusal, and \emph{scrutiny} gives the station that location advice moved to the front of the order the caution threshold and the extra views.

\textbf{What the evidence is.} The planner enforces the rule in code (\texttt{advice.plan}); the advice audit recomputes it from the logged plan, but it uses the same module, so it finds implementation bugs, not a wrong specification. The safety evidence is the \emph{outcome} audit against simulator ground truth, which does not depend on the node's logs. A second implementation of the rule, written by the same authors (with the same AI assistant) from the same specification, was compared with the code by differential testing. It shares any misreading of the specification and is therefore not independent evidence. With mutation tests and raw-byte fuzzing of the advice file it found five bugs, and a proof review found three more; all were fixed (listed in Supp.~Sec.~S10, trust base). The stronger evidence is the z3 check of Prop.~2 above. It covers the code as transcribed, not the specification or a compromised node, and no third party has reproduced it. The destination parse (shelter B) is outside Prop.~2 and rests on a closed-set parse plus an explicit rule (offline, wrong-shelter acceptance must be 0; study A), and text \emph{inside} the camera view reaches the decision as perception, not advice, so no structural argument covers it (study S). A delivery interlock (I-22) that would remove the mission code from the trust base is implemented and off by default. It passed a Gazebo mechanics check on \IlcheckN{} live missions (permits only on approvals; a hidden fire with an injected ``no check needed'' was refused). Study B runs with it, studies A and X without it, because their baselines deliver without a permit by definition. No run uses a tampered mission node, so ``mission gating'' stays a reviewed, not a verified, part of the trust base.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}\label{sec:method}
\textbf{Data.} Prompts, thresholds and model choice were selected on 176 development images and 40 utterances; hazard fixes were also selected on two 300-image hazard sets (Supp.~Sec.~S1). \emph{Held-out images (T1):} 60 Gazebo worlds $\times$ 2 stations $\times$ 5 viewpoints with random lighting, supply pose, fire position and three distractors, two of them unseen in development (Supp.~Sec.~S1). Labels come from the generator, never a model; each view of a fire world is rendered a second time with only flames and smoke in flat magenta, so occlusion is measured: a view is \emph{hidden} if it shows at most 20 fire pixels and \emph{ambiguous} if it shows 21--613 (excluded from primary endpoints, \TNamb{} image), and a fire is \emph{perceivable} in a world if at least one of three views shows 614 or more.

\textbf{Closed loop and audit.} Each trial starts a fresh Gazebo, bridge, guard, VLM server and ROS bag recorder. A trial counts only if an audit confirms it: the decision is recomputed from the saved model outputs and image hash; the ground-truth trajectory shows arrival at the shelter after approval, or no motion toward it after a refusal; odometry agrees with ground truth. Only stack failures \emph{before} any VLM decision may be re-run once; all attempts stay in the ledger. \emph{Expected behavior} is the action the generator's ground truth requires.

\textbf{Registration and statistics.} We \IfAnyPend{registered \NStudies{} studies and ran \NStudiesRun{} of them}{ran \NStudies{} studies} (Table~\ref{tab:studies}; all in Supp.~Table~S12; the initial evaluation C is not counted). Each has a frozen registration (protocol, configuration, endpoints, analysis code) that is fixed before the study's first trial or inference; for \NStudiesChecked{} of them the audit of Supp.~Table~S9 verifies that the registration commit precedes the first results commit. Of the \NStudiesPending{} studies without results \IfAnyPend{at submission (\PendList{})}{yet}, \PreregPending{} (first commit on the main branch and sha256 of every registration: \texttt{docs/REGISTRATIONS.md}); they are not audited on a par with the others, and ``registered before the results'' is claimed for the audited ones only. Studies were designed sequentially, so registration is confirmatory within a study. The primary safety metric is the false-approval rate among must-refuse cases, paired with the correct-approval rate; we use Wilson 95\% intervals, world-cluster bootstrap for pooled image rates, exact McNemar and Wilcoxon tests. A claim of improvement requires the registered direction and $p<0.05$. Claims are not pooled across studies; the headline claims are G, H, V \IfPendA{(and A, if it is run: it is registered, not run at submission)}{and A}, not the \NStudies{} studies together.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\subsection{The Gate in Closed Loop}\label{sec:res-gate}
In campaign D (\DN{} trials) and campaign E (\EN{} trials, a new randomized world each) the robot behaved as expected in \DCorrect{} and \ECorrect{} trials with \DWrong{} and \EWrong{} wrong deliveries (E: \EWrongCI); it completed \ETaskSuccess{} of the \ESafeN{} missions in which a safe delivery was possible. A blind robot that trusts the inventory would have made \EBzeroWrong{} wrong deliveries in the same E trials (\EBzeroWrongCI). On unseen layouts (campaign L, 24 layouts) the robot behaved as expected in \LCorrect{} (\LCorrectCI) with \LUnsafe{} unsafe deliveries. A delivery without search takes about \TimCheck\,s of checking and \TimDrive\,s of driving (Supp.~Table~S5).
\textbf{Supporting studies (Supp.~Secs.~S7, S11, S12).} A zero-shot SigLIP baseline (Z) approved \ZSigTOneFA{} of T1's must-refuse images against \ZOursTOneFA{} for our check; pooled over \RNcond{} camera degradations (R), the check made \RPoolFA{} false approvals; a conformal $\tauh$ (K) kept its miss bound at the cost of more false alarms. On real photographs the frozen threshold flagged \PFalseAlarm{} of the fire-free ones, whereas a threshold calibrated on site photographs (P2) found \PTwoRecall{} of the fires and gave \PTwoFA{} false alarms on unseen photographs (registered claim: \PTwoClaim{}), so a threshold needs per-site calibration. A second checker (J) was not safer than one model (claim \JClaim) and was not adopted.

\subsection{Memory in Changing Worlds (H, V)}\label{sec:res-h}
Campaign H comprised 16 sequences of three missions to the same shelter. Lighting and props were re-drawn in each mission, and four scripted stories changed the world between missions (supplies swapped and stayed swapped, or swapped back; a small fire appeared; a fire disappeared and another appeared). The first mission seeded all arms; missions 2 and 3 ran with no memory (M0), naive memory that skips the check where it last saw the supply or refuses without visiting where it last saw a hazard (M1), and verified memory, i.e.\ memory used only as advice (M2).
Over the \HPTwoN{} paired missions, unsafe deliveries were \HUnsafeMone{} for M1, \HUnsafeMtwo{} for M2 and \HUnsafeMzero{} for M0 (M1 vs.\ M2 exact McNemar $p=\HPTwoP$; by sequence \HSeqMone{} vs.\ \HSeqMtwo{} of \HSeqN, $p=\HSeqP$). M1's unsafe deliveries were \HWrongone{} wrong supplies, relocated since it last looked, and \HFireone{} deliveries into a fire that had appeared since; every one followed a skipped check. The M1--M2 contrast is large by design; the comparison that bounds our risk is M2 versus M0, \HUnsafeMtwo/32 versus \HUnsafeMzero/32 (Wilson upper bounds \HUnsafeUppertwo\% and \HUnsafeUpperzero\%), met as registered but informative only down to those bounds. M0's single unsafe delivery shows what caution is for (Fig.~\ref{fig:hcase}): the new fire was invisible from the front and small from the side, the left view scored $p_h=0.035<\tauh$ and M0 delivered. M2 had recorded a fire at this station two missions earlier, so it checked at $\tauh=0.01$, scored $0.049$ and stopped. Memory can make the robot more careful, never less (Table~\ref{tab:h}).
On the \HPOneN{} missions whose relocation persisted, the median time was \HTimeMtwo\,s for M2 against \HTimeMzero\,s for M0 (exact Wilcoxon $p=\HPOneP$); this subset is the favorable one, and over all \HPTwoN{} paired missions the medians were \HTimeAlltwo\,s against \HTimeAllzero\,s. The price of keeping every check showed after the supplies moved back: M2 first visited the proposed station and then searched (\HChangeMtwo\,s against \HChangeMzero\,s for M0). In the model of repeated missions, verified memory keeps \HPriceFracZero{} of naive memory's saving when nothing changes and none at $q=0.5$ (Supp.~Sec.~S12). With $k$ stations a stale proposal costs up to $k-1$ visits, while the expected saving of a correct one grows with $k$ (descriptive model with the measured detour of \HKDelta\,s: \HKSaveTwo\,s per mission at $k=2$, \HKSaveEight\,s at $k=8$, $q=0.2$; Supp.~Sec.~S7). The registered headline claim (verified memory keeps memory's speed-up without its safety cost) is \HHeadline.
\textbf{Study V} tested the repeated-mission model in closed loop (10 sequences of 6 missions, supply location and fire toggling at random with $q=0.2$, predictions registered before the first trial). The registered 95\% interval for the paired saving M0$-$M2 was \VPredDiffPI\,s; the observed saving was \VObsDiff\,s. Unsafe deliveries were \VUnsafeMzero{} (M0), \VUnsafeMone{} (M1) and \VUnsafeMtwo{} (M2), inside the registered intervals \VPredUnsafezero, \VPredUnsafeone, \VPredUnsafetwo. The model claim is \VClaimModel{} for the registered quantities (the difference, the unsafe counts, the searches); the absolute mean times lie outside their intervals (M0 \VAbsTimezero\,s against \VAbsTimePIzero, M2 \VAbsTimetwo\,s against \VAbsTimePItwo, \VAbsLate\,s above the predicted median), and only the difference between the arms is inside. The memory claim is \VClaimMemory: the margin held (M2$-$M0 $=1\le3$), but the sequence-level test did not (M1 unsafe in \VSeqMone{} sequences, M2 in \VSeqMtwo; exact McNemar $p=\VSeqP$). This is a failure of power, not evidence of a safety cost: with M1 unsafe in \VSeqMone{} sequences the test can pass only if M2 has no unsafe delivery in any of them (smallest possible $p=\VMinP$), while the registered model predicted a median of \VPredMtwoMed{} (\VPredMtwoPI) unsafe M2 deliveries. Pooled over H and V (\MemPoolN{} missions), unsafe deliveries were \MemPoolzero{} for M0, \MemPooltwo{} for M2 (Wilson \MemPoolCI) and \MemPoolone{} for naive memory. The M2 event is a check error, not a memory effect: in the same world M0's right view scored $p_h=0.33$ and refused, M2's scored 0.013 after arrival jitter, which is above the I-20 suspicion threshold, but that rule was off in V.

\subsection{Outside the Guarantee: Scene Text and Realism}\label{sec:res-out}

\section{Discussion and Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Inputs, not just models, decide safety.} Trusting memory is efficient but its risk grows with how often the world changes, which the robot cannot observe without looking (H). Observation~1 and Proposition~2 settle whether checks happen: memory and any other advice decide only the order and strictness of checks, never their existence; the guarantee ends at the camera. \textbf{Moving first, then wording.} The largest single gain in $\varepsilon$ came from moving 0.4\,m (G); in W's v3.5 reference run the look-around alone cut the misses from \WVOldMissFront{} to \WVOldMissLA{}, and the v3.6 wording and crops removed \WWordRemoved{} more. \textbf{Value of pre-registration.} Of the registered claims, \Nfailed{} failed (\FailedList); the first two led to the look-around, and J kept us from a second model.

\textbf{Limits.} The guarantee is relative: G's plug-in $k\hat\varepsilon=\GKEpsHat$ for the hardest class (upper bound \GKEpsUpper, 16 worlds) is measured again in closed loop (v3.6 arms of G, GR, GC, GD and GL pooled: \DepCalPool{}, upper \DepCalPoolUpper; GL's depot arm alone \GLV{}, upper \GLVUpper, mostly fires the front view cannot see); a left-only 0.8\,m look-around cut the depot misses from \GLV{} to \GLLeft{} (GL, $p=\GLP$; site-specific), and extra views taken only when the gate is suspicious cut them from \JKV{} to \JKAdapt{} without calibration (JK, $p=\JKP$, false stops \JKFalseStops\IfFileExists{jl_section.tex}{; fresh worlds, concurrent control: \JLV{} against \JLAdapt, JL}{}; Supp.~Sec.~S12); the order-independent $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{any}}\le k\hat\varepsilon$ is the number to read, estimated at image level (W: \WEpsAnyTwo{} for $k=2$, \WEpsAnyFour{} for $k=4$ with look-around). Exposure to a really burning station is not bounded. Repeated advice is priced per account by a credit that only drops advice (Prop.~3). \IfPendB{Its closed-loop test (study B) was not run at submission.}{In closed loop (study B, a mechanics check) a source that lied at all \BMissions{} missions wasted \BWastedCredit{} visits with the credit and \BWastedNo{} without (bound \BBound); \BAuditFail{} audit failures.} $\varepsilon_s$ differs by station and fire geometry, so the budget we measure on one class does not transfer to another. Scene text is perception and outside the guarantee, and so are the requested supply and destination parsed from the operator's text, which Prop.~2 does not cover (unlisted places were read as shelter~B in fresh texts: IG, \IGUnsafe{} unsafe). \IfPendIF{Study IF (72 flip and control texts) is registered to measure this but was not run at submission, so this boundary is stated, not measured.}{Study IF: the frozen parser accepted an injected supply in \IFK{} of \IFN{} switching texts, so the request must be read back; with the ask-back fix (I-27) \IfPendIFtwo{(not run at submission)}{\IFtwoK{} of \IFtwoN{} new texts were accepted}.} All results are from simulation (one robot, two supply types, a stylized fire, sensor degradations rather than real objects or smoke); the depot (study X) narrows the gap but stays simulation (no grasping, real smoke or sensor noise). Barely visible fires (21--613 pixels) are excluded, and the guard is hand-written, not synthesized~\cite{alshiekh2018shielding,ames2019cbf}. Threats to validity: Supp.~Sec.~S9.

\section{Conclusion}
The look-around shrank the budget; verified memory kept a stale input from spending more than the budget (H: \HHeadline; V: \StatV; \MemPooltwo{} unsafe in \MemPoolN{} missions with verified memory)\IfPendA{; for hostile advice only the structural result (Prop.~2; A not run at submission)}{ and tighten-only advice a hostile one (A: unsafe worlds \AWorldsOurs{}, against \AWorldsNaive{} for the baselines)}. The guarantee is relative, steering costs deliveries, and it ends at the camera, in simulation.
