\subsection{Study IJ: An Answerability Probe for Unknown Destinations}\label{sec:moved-ij}
IG and IH showed that the parser reads requests to unknown places as requests to shelter~B, and that neither a list rule nor the model's own destination probability separates them. Probes on hidden states can detect unanswerable or out-of-scope inputs where output probabilities fail~\cite{wagner2026twoaxes,lavi2025linear}. Study IJ (registered and stamped before any feature was extracted) fits a logistic probe on the frozen Qwen3.5-9B's hidden states (layer \IJLayer{} chosen on development data by leave-one-family-out AUROC) to flag requests to unknown destinations; \IJDev{} development and \IJTest{} test texts, five whole place-name families held out for test. Thresholds come from the development negatives by the rank rule.
\emph{Claims.} IJ1: probe AUROC \IJProbeAuroc{} (\IJProbeAurocCI, family bootstrap) at least 0.05 above the better of the model's zero-shot yes-probability (\IJZeroShotAuroc) and its closed-set probability of ``other'' (\IJPOtherAuroc): \IJOneHolds. IJ2: at most 15\% of unknown-destination requests pass the probe (\IJProbePass): \IJTwoHolds. IJ3: at most 8\% of listed-destination requests refused (\IJProbeFalseRef): \IJThreeHolds. Baselines: difference of means \IJDiffMeansAuroc{} (pass-through \IJDiffMeansPass), list rule pass-through \IJListRulePass. Predictions: the probe passes fewer than the list rule \IJPredOneHolds; at least 4 of 5 test families pass through at most 25\% \IJPredTwoHolds.
\emph{Reading.} (Written after the numbers: whether the internal state knows what the output hides, how the probe fails on unseen families, and why it is not integrated: a passing probe is a candidate for a registered closed-loop test of the parser, while the read-back of the parsed supply and destination stays mandatory.)
