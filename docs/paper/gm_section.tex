\subsection{Study GM: Does the Left-Only Look-Around Transfer to Other Depot Renderings?}\label{sec:moved-gm}
GL is the paper's only closed-loop miss reduction, and it was fitted to the generator that produced the idea. Study GM (registered and stamped before any design mask; \GMTrials{} runs) asks whether it survives a change of rendering: every world has one realism feature of XD's ablation set switched off (default lighting, no clutter, no surroundings, or plain platforms, in a fixed cycle by world number), the threshold is \emph{not} recalibrated ($\tau_h=0.028$ of GL), and the worlds are fresh (\GMWorlds{} fire worlds and \GMPlain{} fire-free worlds, two arms each: \texttt{v36} and \texttt{left}).
\emph{Claims.} GM1 (primary): the exact one-sided McNemar test gives $p<0.05$ and fewer deliveries into fire: \texttt{v36} \GMV{} against \texttt{left} \GMLeft{}, \GMOnlyV{} worlds unsafe only under \texttt{v36} against \GMOnlyLeft{}, $p=\GMP$: \GMOneHolds. GM2: \texttt{left} upper bound at most 15\% (\GMLeftRate, upper \GMLeftUpper): \GMTwoHolds. GM3: at most 3 of the fire-free worlds refused for a hazard, \GMFalseStops{} (\texttt{v36}: \GMFalseStopsV): \GMThreeHolds. Median mission time \GMTimeV\,s (\texttt{v36}) against \GMTimeLeft\,s (\texttt{left}). Per variant (fire worlds unsafe, \texttt{v36} against \texttt{left}; false stops of \texttt{left}): default lighting \GMLightV{} / \GMLightLeft{} ($\,$\GMLightFS), no clutter \GMClutterV{} / \GMClutterLeft{} (\GMClutterFS), no surroundings \GMSurrV{} / \GMSurrLeft{} (\GMSurrFS), plain platforms \GMPlatV{} / \GMPlatLeft{} (\GMPlatFS).
\emph{Reading.} The reduction of GL transferred. With the threshold borrowed unchanged, the left-only arm delivered into the fire in \GMLeft{} fire worlds (upper bound \GMLeftUpper, inside the registered 15\% line) against \GMV{} for \texttt{v36}, and the pairing is one-sided but not overwhelming ($p=\GMP$; the effect is smaller than GL's). It points the same way in all four variants, and it is largest where the surroundings are removed (\GMSurrV{} to \GMSurrLeft{}): the bay is then the only structure the camera sees, which is also the setting in which the front view hides the fire best. The clean tail did not move under the shifted renderings: neither arm stopped any of the \GMPlain{} fire-free worlds (\GMFalseStops), so the borrowed $\tau_h=0.028$ held without recalibration here, as GL's own calibration promised only for the standard rendering. The left-only arm is also faster (median \GMTimeLeft\,s against \GMTimeV\,s) because it drives one peek instead of two. Limits: simulation; the same crates and fire geometry as GL; four removals of detail, each with 24--26 fire worlds, so no variant alone is conclusive; two fires (GM031, GM079) are missed by both arms. The arm stays a \emph{site-specific option} whose threshold needs the site's own clean views (GD, GV); GM shows that a change in rendering, by itself, does not undo it. Robustness studies of vision-language models find that spatial distortions hurt more than photometric changes~\cite{saxena2026vlmrobustbench}, that most models are thrown by distracting background context~\cite{patel2025pcri}, and that corruptions can lower reliability without lowering accuracy~\cite{usama2025corruptions}; hazard perception under degraded firefighting video is hard for models~\cite{tiwari2025fire360}. Of the four removals in GM, the ones that matter for the arms are surroundings (\GMSurrV{} to \GMSurrLeft{}). A factor the worlds vary at random and that we can read post hoc is the smoke plume of the fire (\texttt{scripts/smoke\_strata.py}): of the \SMV{} fire worlds with a particle plume and \SMNoV{} without, v3.6 delivered into \SMVSmoke{} against \SMVNoSmoke{}, the left-only arm \SMLeftSmoke{} against \SMLeftNoSmoke{}, and in JK the extra views \SMAdaptSmoke{} against \SMAdaptNoSmoke{}: every residual miss of the two best arms is in a smoke world (Fisher exact $p=\SMLeftP$ and $\SMAdaptP$ for the two arms; descriptive). Haze or smoke density is therefore the environment factor not yet varied on purpose and the one most worth a registered ablation.
