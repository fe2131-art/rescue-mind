\subsection{Multiplicity, Reused Worlds and Infrastructure (post hoc)}\label{sec:moved-mult}
Forty-odd pre-registered studies at $p<0.05$ invite the question of what the claims are worth together. We declare one family after the fact: the primary paired closed-loop test of every study that registered one (A, F, G, GR, GC, GD, GL, GM, H, V, J, JK\IfFileExists{jl_section.tex}{ and JL}{}; \texttt{scripts/multiplicity.py}; one-sided where the study registered a direction), \MultM{} tests. \MultRaw{} reach $p<0.05$ uncorrected; after Holm--Bonferroni at a family-wise 5\% \MultHolm{} remain, and with Benjamini--Hochberg at a false-discovery rate of 5\% \MultBH{}. The test lost by Holm is \MultLost{} ($p=\MultGMP$, Holm-adjusted $\MultGMHolm$): the replication of GL under changed rendering is the weakest of the closed-loop reductions, which is how the paper reads it. The strongest remain far below any correction: GR ($p=\MultGRP$, adjusted \MultGRHolm), GL (\MultGLP, \MultGLHolm), JK (\MultJKP, \MultJKHolm) and G (\MultGP, \MultGHolm). The correction treats the tests as exchangeable p-values and does not model that GM and JK share worlds (JK reuses GM's worlds against GM's own earlier runs); that is the reason for the fresh-world, interleaved replication JL, which is the one confirmatory test of the extra views whose worlds and control are not reused. Reusing a test set adaptively inflates apparent gains~\cite{blum2015ladder}, and many pairwise claims on shared items depend on how pairing and multiplicity are handled~\cite{reyes2026multiplecomparison}; the registration commits and external timestamps are the paper's answer to researcher degrees of freedom, and its limits are the ones discussed for preregistration in NLP~\cite{sogaard2023twosided}.
\emph{Simulation as evidence.} Every claim is conditional on the simulator: simulation-based safety assurance with calibrated thresholds has formal precedents~\cite{luo2021conformalsim}, but combining many simulated tests with a few real ones to correct simulator bias~\cite{badithela2025suresim} is exactly what we could not do (no real robot). Simulators are also known to be flaky and hard to reproduce~\cite{amini2023flaky,afzal2020challenges}; our practice is a fixed protocol and seed order, a rerun of a run that failed before any model decision, a post-run audit against ground truth, and a pilot before each campaign (the JK pilot found three defects before its first trial, Amendments 1--3 of its registration). The rerun-once rule does not detect flakiness that changes an outcome without a failure; we did not repeat runs to measure it.
