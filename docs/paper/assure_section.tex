\subsection{The Guarantee as an Assurance Argument, and Reading the Scoring Method Critically (post hoc)}\label{sec:moved-assure}
\textbf{Formal neighbours.} Bounds on unsafe outcomes with imperfect perception exist: shields built on confidence intervals of a learned perception's error rates give high-probability finite-horizon guarantees~\cite{scarbro2026intervalpomdp}, conservative interval-MDP models of closed loops with perception hold at a user-set probability~\cite{cleaveland2025conservative}, a split of a policy into a large controller and a small verifiable safety module~\cite{corsi2026fearl}, and a small runtime monitor that checks evidence from an untrusted controller~\cite{jackson2021certifiedcontrol}. Our Proposition~2 differs in what it bounds and by what: unsafe delivery by the \emph{gate's own} error budget under advice that may only reorder visits or tighten thresholds, with the proof machine-checked against a transcription of the deployed gate code (the proof section of this supplement); it does not bound perception error, which is measured, and it is conditional on the assumptions below.
\textbf{Argument with defeaters.} In the manner of assurance cases for machine-learning components~\cite{hawkins2021amlas}, the top claim is the bound of Proposition~2. Its evidence nodes are the proof, the 9{,}000 model-to-code comparisons and the 11 of 11 planted bugs detected (which shows the conformance check has power, not that no defect remains). Its assumptions are A1 (frames are exogenous), A2 (the plan only tightens), A3 (delivery only after the gate approved the captured frames) and A4 (advice does not influence the world or the model), plus exchangeability of calibration and deployment worlds for any threshold calibrated per site. Each carries a defeater, stated as such~\cite{gohar2026defeaters}: A1 fails if a visit changes what a later frame shows (a robot that pushes the shelf), A2 if memory is trusted naively, A3 if the interlock is off and a code path bypasses the gate, A4 if the adversary reaches the simulator or the weights, exchangeability if the site is not the calibration site (it failed at the second site for the miss rate, GD). The deployment guide's list of what was not validated is the list of assurance deficits.
\textbf{Reading the scoring method critically.} Calibrated verbalised confidence has been reported to beat token probabilities on question answering~\cite{tian2023justask}; we use token probabilities because they are available in one forward pass, can be thresholded per site by a rank rule, and give a graded score, and we did not compare verbalised confidence. Option-letter and position bias is documented for vision-language models~\cite{atabuzzaman2025mcqabias}, and answers move with lexically altered prompts~\cite{dumpala2024promptsensitivity}; the gate asks the supply question twice with permuted options and requires both. On the saved gate outputs (\texttt{scripts/permutation\_agreement.py}) the two passes name different supplies on \PAXDDisagree{} of \PAXDN{} XD images and \PAJIDisagree{} of \PAJIN{} JI images; accuracy against the truth is \PAXDOne{} for the first pass and \PAXDTwo{} for the second on XD (JI: \PAJIOne{} and \PAJITwo{}), the second pass, in which water moves to the last position, being the weaker one, consistent with an option-position effect of about one point that the conjunction absorbs. Yes/no probabilities of a VLM as a score are established practice for existence questions~\cite{li2023pope} and for image-text matching~\cite{lin2024vqascore}.
\textbf{The read-back is untested.} Human oversight is not free: simple oversight protocols need not beat each other~\cite{recchia2025confirmation}, over-reliance on an AI recommendation is measurable~\cite{horowitz2023automationbias}, and a model of reviewer fatigue finds that more escalation can lower safety~\cite{turan2026oversight}. Our deployment guide makes the operator's read-back of the parsed supply and destination mandatory, and we did not test whether operators notice a parsing error in it. A small web-form study (typed requests with parsed fields, injected errors, a forced match/mismatch choice per field against a plain confirm button; detection of at least 80\% of injected errors at at most 10\% false alarms as the line) is the registered-style next step; it needs participants and is not run.
