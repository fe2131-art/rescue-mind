# Pre-registration — study QI (Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo: photo-realised and edited images, frozen gate), fixed before any generated image is scored

The registration is the commit that adds this file together with `scripts/qi_generate.py`, `scripts/qi_build_devset.py`, `scripts/qi_analysis.py` and `tests/test_qi_generate.py`, **before any generated image is scored by the gate**
(`study QI: registration`; stamped with OpenTimestamps, hashes only). Generating images uses no gate score; a pilot of at most 20 images per mode (generated before this commit) fixes only the numeric precision (fp16 or bf16) and may refit the pixel check `FIRE_MIN`; its images are not part of the study.
Design note: `docs/QWENIMAGE_DESIGN.md` (idea: the user). Study GL must be finished before generation starts (one GPU, one lock, GL forbids downloads and other GPU work while it runs).

## What QI asks
The paper's weakest points are the simulator-to-reality gap and the small hard-class sample. The gate (Qwen3.5-9B, v3.6: h36 + tiles, both passes, fail-closed) and its threshold are **frozen**; nothing is fitted on any QI image.
1. **Does the gate still catch the simulator's hard fires when the frame is photo-realised?** (QI-R)
2. **Does it catch a fire that a generative image model draws, behind the same crates, in the clean twin of an XD fire world?** (QI-G)
3. **Do the generative pixels themselves create false alarms?** (QI-R clean worlds; QI-C controls)

## Image sets (all from `experiments/vlm_testset_xd`, 640 x 480; the generator works at 1024 x 768 and the result is resized back)
* **QI-R:** every XD **test** image with view F, L or R (768 images; 126 perceivable hard-class fire worlds, 104 clean worlds counted as in JH), photo-realised with one fixed prompt (`PROMPTS['R']`), seed 20261022 + index. Ground truth = the XD label.
* **QI-G:** the clean twin of every XD fire world that has a fire mask for the view (test split: 44 worlds, 205 images), the fire pixels edited **only inside the fire world's own dilated fire mask** (12 px); every pixel outside is the twin's own byte (checked). Ground truth = fire (by construction).
* **QI-C:** the same twins and masks, a neutral prompt that adds nothing: the pipeline's artifacts alone (clean by construction).
Pixel check (from pixels only, never from a gate score): a QI-G image is **excluded and listed** if pixels outside the mask changed or the flame-coloured share of the mask is below `FIRE_MIN` = 0.02 (an edit without any did not draw a fire); QI-C: pixels outside the mask changed. QI-R has no pixel check: the simulator's own fires are rarely flame-coloured (median share 0 for small tongues), so a realised frame in which the editor removed the fire cannot be told from a miss; this limit is stated, and sample pairs are inspected by eye (descriptive, no exclusion). More than 15% excluded images in QI-G stops the QI-G claim (then it is "the editor does not draw the fire reliably").

## Score and threshold
World score = largest hazard score (both passes, fail-closed) over F, L, R (the rule of XD and JH). **tau = 0.088** (XD rank rule at alpha = 2% on its clean calibration worlds; 0.05, the v3.6 default, is reported). The scoring path is `scripts/vlm_devset_eval.py` with the XD tag settings. The analysis first checks its own baseline: XD's original scores must give **14 of 126 misses and 0 of 104 false stops** (the JH numbers); a different baseline means the scoring path differs and nothing is claimed.

## Claims (`scripts/qi_analysis.py`)
* **QI1 (realism transfer).** On QI-R the miss on the 126 perceivable hard-class worlds is **at most 20** (XD's own: 14; the tolerance is about 5 points).
* **QI2 (a drawn fire).** On QI-G the miss rate over the generated fire worlds is **at most 20%** (n <= 44; reported with the exact one-sided 95% upper bound).
* **QI3 (no new false alarms).** On QI-R the realised clean worlds give **at most 5 false stops of 104** (XD's own: 0); on QI-C at most **5%** of the control-edited twins cross tau.
A failed claim goes to `failed_claims`. Descriptive (predictions registered now, failing them is reported): P1 realised frames lower the miss or leave it (the gate does not depend on the simulator's rendering style); P2 generated fires are easier than the simulator's small tongues (miss rate below 14/126); P3 the three families of study JH agree with the gate on QI-G (a gap would show a circular advantage, since the generator's text encoder is Qwen3-VL, the gate's own family).

## Known limits, stated now
* Generated images are not real photographs; "more realistic and different" is the claim, not "real". The generator is of the gate's family (circularity); P3 and the JH judges are the check.
* QI-G worlds are the 44 XD test fire worlds with twins (same geometry as XD), so the independent sample is not larger than XD's; the study measures a different rendering, not a bigger sample.
* The tolerances (20 misses, 20%, 5) are set before any generated image is scored; they are not derived from a power calculation.
* One generator checkpoint (Turbo, 8 steps), one prompt per mode, one seed per image.

## Amendment 1 (2026-10-23; before any generated image was scored and before any QI image of a study set exists)
Made while the pilot ran, with no gate score seen. (a) **Mask bug:** the files in `fire_masks/` are renders of the fire alone (fire = magenta key colour), not binary masks; the first G pilot read the whole frame as the fire region. `scripts/qi_generate.py` now uses the colour rule of `scripts/fire_mask_at_pose.py` (`fire_pixels_of`; test added); the three G pilot images were regenerated and kept the registered FIRE_MIN = 0.02 (flame-coloured share of the mask 0.000, 0.022, 0.057 for F, L, R of one twin: F would be excluded and listed). (b) **R prompt:** the registered R prompt changed the picture by a mean absolute difference of about 4 grey levels (three images: the realised frame is almost the simulator frame), which would make QI1 and QI3 vacuous. Two stronger prompts were tried on three images (a clean medicine world, a clean water world, a fire world; `outputs/qi/R_pR2`, `R_pR3`, manifests `qi_manifest_R_pR*.jsonl`) and compared by eye for materials (fibres, shrink-wrap, scuffed steel, concrete) against preserved layout, objects and fire; **no gate score was used.** The stronger materials prompt (R3: "Convert this 3D-rendered image into a photograph shot on a real camera ...") is now `PROMPTS['R']`; the registered one is kept as `PROMPTS['R0']`, the other as `R2`, for provenance. Claims, thresholds and the other modes are unchanged. Cost: about 40 s per image (8 steps, fp16 on the V100, CPU offload), about 1,180 images, about 13 h.

## Amendment 3 (2026-10-23; reviewer check, before any QI-G, QI-C or extension image exists and before any QI score)
(a) **World coverage.** A world score needs F, L and R. The generated modes only exist for the views in which the fire world's fire is visible (65 of 72 twin worlds have all three; 4 have one, 3 have two), and the pixel check can exclude more (the pilot's F view of one twin drew no flame). Scoring only worlds with all three views would drop the side-only and the hardest-to-draw fires, which are exactly the cases that matter. **Rule (fixed now):** for G, G2, C, K1 and K2 a view that was not generated or was excluded keeps the clean XD twin's own unedited score ("no fire drawn there"); the world score is the maximum over F, L, R as before. A world with no generated view at all is not scored and is counted and listed (generation failure, not a miss). `qi_summary.json` reports both the filled count and the strict count (worlds with all three generated views) under `coverage`, and QI2 is read on the filled set, with the strict set shown next to it. (b) **Did the realiser keep the fire?** `scripts/qi_r_audit.py` (descriptive, pixels only): per R fire image the flame-colour share inside the XD design fire render before and after, and the mean change inside it; every realised fire world that the gate misses is also looked at by eye and classed "fire erased" or "fire present, missed" in FINDINGS. QI1 is read together with this audit: a miss with an erased fire counts against the generator, not the gate (reported, the claim stays as registered). Claims, thresholds and prompts are unchanged.

## Amendment 4 (2026-10-23; written after looking at 165 realised fire images, before the R set is complete and before any QI score)
Looked at by eye (not scored): the simulator's hard fires are mostly **grey translucent smoke domes** with almost no flame colour, so the flame-colour audit of Amendment 3 is vacuous for them (flame share 0 before the edit). The realiser **erased the dome in several of the eight images checked** (the grey blob is gone; in one it became a solid dark ball). A realised "fire world" whose fire is gone is not a fire world, so QI1 as registered (at most 20 misses of 126, ground truth = the XD label) would partly measure the generator. Changes: (a) the audit now uses the **contrast** of the fire render's region against a 15 px ring (luminance, before/after) when the flame share is under 0.02; `kept` = the contrast keeps its sign and at least half its size (`scripts/qi_r_audit.py`; 142 of 165 audited images kept, 23 not, on the images generated so far). (b) `qi_analysis.py` reports, next to the registered QI1 count on all 126 worlds (**unchanged, and still the claim that goes to `failed_claims` if it fails**), the miss on the worlds whose fire is kept (a world counts as lost when none of its views with at least 614 fire pixels is kept) and the list of lost worlds that the gate misses; the companion QI1k (rate on kept worlds at most 20/126 = 15.9%) is descriptive. (c) The paper says in plain words that the generator removed the simulator's smoke domes in a share of the images, which is a finding about the simulator's non-photoreal smoke as much as about the gate. Prompts, thresholds and the other claims are unchanged.

## Amendment 5 (2026-10-23 05:55; before any gate score on QI-G was read) — the registered QI-G stop rule is now computed
`scripts/qi_build_devset.py` excluded **140 of the 205 generated fire images (68%)** by the registered pixel check (flame-coloured share of the mask below 0.02). The registration says that more than 15% excluded images stops the QI-G claim ("the editor does not draw the fire reliably"). `scripts/qi_analysis.py` now computes this stop rule (`G.excluded`, `G.claim_stopped`) and `QI2` cannot hold when it is triggered; the miss rate on the 65 remaining images is reported as descriptive only. The registered QI2 therefore fails by the stop rule, whatever the gate scores. Thresholds, prompts and the other claims are unchanged. A by-eye look (no scores) at 24 random G images had already shown about a third without any drawn fire.
